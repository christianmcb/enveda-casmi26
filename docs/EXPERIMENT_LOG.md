### v0.2b — Finer m/z bins, 0.1 Da, sparse (P4b, 2026-10-20)
- Hypothesis: a 1 Da bin discards each fragment's mass defect (digits after the decimal point), which encodes elemental composition; 0.1 Da bins keep part of it.
- Single change: bin width of BOTH blocks (fragments and neutral losses) 1.0 -> 0.1 Da (10,000 columns per block, 19,600 non-empty after dropping empty columns; sparse, float-edge guard eps=1e-6). Ridge alpha 1.0 unchanged, solved with the normal equations (NormalEqRidge); solver check on the 1 Da features reproduces sklearn exactly (0.4469 / 0.2767, max prediction difference 0.002).
- Benchmark (230k pool). enveda-180 val (primary, n=2,818): model **0.3214** | random 0.1835 | model-minus-random 0.1378 | median cands 33  (v0.2a: 0.2767 | 0.1835 | 0.0932 | 33).
  Mixed val (n=2,000, 10 ppm): model **0.4839** | random 0.3111 | gap 0.1727 | median cands 15  (v0.2a: 0.4469 | 0.3111 | 0.1358 | 15). enveda-180 at 10 ppm: 0.2389 vs 0.1884.
- Paired molecule-clustered bootstrap, 95% CI of the gain vs v0.2a: enveda-180 +0.0447 [+0.0348, +0.0545]; mixed +0.0370 [+0.0265, +0.0477].
- Diagnostics (not versions), enveda-180 / mixed: 0.5 Da both 0.2987 / 0.4716; 0.1 Da fragments only 0.3095 / 0.4762; 0.1 Da losses only 0.2972 / 0.4726 (gains roughly additive); alpha sweep at 0.1 Da: a=1 0.3214, 3 0.3175, 10 0.3149, 30 0.3064, 100 0.2890 (mixed best at a=3: 0.4848, same as a=1 within noise). Not a regularisation artefact; the score rose monotonically with resolution (1 -> 0.5 -> 0.1 Da).
- Notes / surprises: (1) My first explanation, merged peaks inside one 1 Da bin, is NOT supported: such merged peaks are rare and faint (12% of occupied bins merge peaks >=0.1 Da apart, but almost all faint). The mass-defect explanation fits: intense fragment defects lie in about [-0.05, +0.25] and grow with mass, so 0.1 Da bins resolve only ~2-3 levels of it. (2) The gain curve was still rising at the finest resolution tested; 0.01-0.05 Da bins are untestable with normal equations (p would be 40k-200k). (3) Only 2.9% of intense peaks sit within 10 mDa of an integer edge. (4) Whole-fingerprint cosine again barely moves (0.5065), so gains are in ranking near-identical candidates. (5) Bugs caught by checks while building: float32/float64 mismatch made the Cholesky fail; cache key lacked the solver; float noise at bin edges (303.1-203.1 = 99.99999999999997).
- Decision: adopt as the new baseline (v0.2b). P4 complete. Next candidates: P4c (mass-defect-aware sub-bins), P5 (model that can use sparse high-resolution input).

### v0.2c — Mass-defect-aware sub-bins (P4c, 2026-10-20)
- Hypothesis: fragment mass defects live in a narrow band ([-0.05, +0.25) holds 98% of intense test peaks), so spending columns as 0.02 Da sub-bins inside that band beats uniform 0.1 Da bins.
- Single change: binning scheme of BOTH blocks. Per nominal mass n = floor(x + 0.05): 15 sub-bins of 0.02 Da on defect [-0.05, +0.25) + 1 overflow column (16 per Da; 32,019 columns, 28,317 non-empty). Ridge alpha 1.0, pool, windows, eval identical to v0.2b (reference reproduced exactly with the rewritten solver: 0.3214 / 0.4839).
- Benchmark (230k pool). enveda-180 val (primary, n=2,818): model **0.4084** | random 0.1835 | model-minus-random 0.2249 | median cands 33  (v0.2b: 0.3214 | 0.1835 | 0.1378 | 33).
  Mixed val (n=2,000, 10 ppm): model **0.5425** | random 0.3111 | gap 0.2314 | median cands 15  (v0.2b: 0.4839 | 0.3111 | 0.1727 | 15). enveda-180 at 10 ppm: 0.3464 vs 0.2389.
- Paired molecule-clustered bootstrap, 95% CI of the gain vs v0.2b: enveda-180 +0.0870 [+0.0739, +0.1007]; mixed +0.0586 [+0.0463, +0.0711].
- Ladder (enveda-180 / mixed): defect 0.05 both 0.3563 / 0.5139; defect 0.02 fragments only (loss uniform 0.1) 0.3934 / 0.5255; defect 0.02 both 0.4084 / 0.5425. Monotone in resolution; losses add +0.015 on top of fragments.
- Checks: (1) alpha 1 / 3 / 10 -> 0.4084 / 0.4118 / 0.4070 (enveda-180), 0.5425 / 0.5495 / 0.5441 (mixed): not a regularisation artefact, alpha 1 stays official. (2) Gain by source (all val spectra, 10 ppm window): enveda-180 +0.108, pluskal_ms2 +0.106, gnps +0.053, mona +0.040, spectraverse +0.027, massbank +0.026, riken +0.015, msdial -0.000, drug_plus -0.000 (n=82); ALL +0.0655 [+0.059, +0.071]. (3) Memorisation check, gain by max Tanimoto of the val molecule to any training molecule: enveda-180 0.3-0.4 +0.084 [+0.060, +0.109], 0.4-0.5 +0.090, 0.5-0.6 +0.165, >=0.6 +0.160; non-enveda-180 <0.3 +0.039 [+0.017, +0.065], 0.3-0.4 +0.043, 0.4-0.5 +0.074, 0.5-0.6 +0.082, >=0.6 +0.034. So part of the gain depends on a similar molecule being in train, but a substantial gain remains for dissimilar molecules. Natural-product test molecules should look like the low-similarity bins: expect roughly +0.04 to +0.08, NOT the +0.087 headline.
- Notes / surprises: (1) Whole-fingerprint cosine FELL (0.5065 -> 0.4870 enveda-180; 0.5140 -> 0.5000 mixed) while MRR rose, and it rises with alpha (0.4870 / 0.5048 / 0.5262 at alpha 1 / 3 / 10) while MRR is flat: bit-level accuracy and ranking among isomer candidates are different objectives; track both in P5. (2) np-examples has too few val spectra (<30) for a per-source estimate, so the natural-product-like source itself is untested. (3) The 6.4 GB Gram matrix is consumed by the solve (no cheap alpha refits) and a single process running several large fits was killed under memory pressure: run each fit in its own process (work/p4c_fit.py). (4) Defect sub-bins at 0.02 Da cost 32k columns, the ceiling of the normal-equation solver; finer needs a different learner (P5).
- Decision: adopt as the new baseline (v0.2c). P4 complete. Next: P5 (learner that can use sparse high-resolution input), tracking both MRR and fingerprint accuracy.

### v0.3 (NOT ADOPTED AT 62k SPECTRA; SUPERSEDED by v0.3b below) — Ridge -> MLP on the v0.2c features (P5, 2026-10-20)
- Hypothesis: spectra -> substructure mapping has peak interactions a linear model cannot capture, so an MLP with a BCE loss beats Ridge.
- Single change: the learner. MLP (input 28,317 sparse columns -> hidden -> hidden -> 1,024 sigmoid outputs), BCE, AdamW lr 1e-3 wd 1e-2, batch 256, seed 42, ~11 s/epoch on MPS. Early stopping (patience 6) on a scaffold-grouped 10% holdout INSIDE the training split; four configs compared by inner-holdout BCE only (1024x2 d0.5: 0.12278 [official]; 2048x1024 d0.5: 0.12291; 1024x2 d0.3: 0.12320; 512x2 d0.2: 0.12400); best epochs 5-6, i.e. quick overfitting. Features, targets, pool, windows, cosine ranking unchanged.
- Benchmark (230k pool), official MLP vs v0.2c Ridge. enveda-180 val (primary): MLP 0.2832 | random 0.1835 | gap 0.0997 | median cands 33  vs Ridge 0.4084 | 0.1835 | 0.2249 | 33. Mixed val: MLP 0.4542 | gap 0.1431 vs Ridge 0.5425 | 0.2314.
- Paired molecule-clustered bootstrap, MLP minus Ridge: enveda-180 -0.1252 [-0.1397, -0.1101]; mixed -0.0883 [-0.1021, -0.0738]; all val -0.0902. All four configs lose by 0.11-0.13 (enveda-180). Best config by val (not used for selection): 2048x1024 d0.5, 0.3000 / 0.4680.
- Where it loses (official MLP minus Ridge, by max Tanimoto of the val molecule to train): enveda-180 -0.085 (0.3-0.4), -0.112 (0.4-0.5), -0.230 (0.5-0.6), -0.221 (>=0.6); non-enveda -0.017 [-0.045, +0.009] (<0.3, n=301), -0.046 (0.3-0.4), -0.098, -0.114, -0.066. The gap closes for dissimilar molecules: Ridge's advantage is largest where a similar molecule is in train (sharp lookup of exact fragment masses).
- Bit-level quality is NOT worse: whole-fingerprint cosine 0.538 vs 0.487 (enveda-180), mean per-bit AUC on all val 0.720 vs 0.677 (MLP better), but on enveda-180 val 0.609 vs 0.630 (Ridge better).
- Rescue attempts (diagnostics, declared before looking, no tuning): (a) 50/50 average of Ridge and MLP probabilities: 0.3540 (w=0.25: 0.3186; w=0.75: 0.3832) vs clipped-Ridge 0.4011; every blend worse, monotone in Ridge weight. (b) Bernoulli log-likelihood ranking (rank by sum candidate_bit * logit(p), p clipped to [1e-4, 1-1e-4]): Ridge 0.4084 -> 0.3755 (-0.033 [-0.046, -0.019]); MLP 0.2832 -> 0.2454 (-0.038 [-0.052, -0.023]). Cosine wins for both models, so the loss is not caused by the ranking rule. (c) 2x2 on enveda-180: Ridge+cosine 0.4084, Ridge+loglik 0.3755, MLP+cosine 0.2832, MLP+loglik 0.2454.
- Notes / surprises: (1) Clipping Ridge outputs to [0,1] costs it 0.007 (0.4011 vs 0.4084): its negative outputs carry ranking information, so do not "calibrate" Ridge before cosine ranking. (2) Early stopping on BCE is a bit-level criterion dominated by common bits; it may stop before the MLP learns the rare, sharp fragment-mass features that matter for discriminating isomers. That explanation is UNTESTED (an MRR-aligned inner-holdout stopping rule would test it). (3) PyTorch 2.14.1 runs from a scratch install (not the global environment) with MPS.
- Decision: do NOT adopt; v0.2c Ridge remains the baseline. P5 closed as a negative result. The MLP's better bit-level AUC on the mixed set suggests it may still be useful as an extra FEATURE for a learned reranker (P6) rather than as the main ranker.

### v0.3b — 10x more training data + deep residual MLP (P5b learning-curve test, 2026-10-20)
- Hypothesis (Christian's question): the MLP lost in v0.3 because the training set was tiny (61,701 spectra = 3.6% of the cleaned data, 29,964 molecules) and/or the net was too shallow, not because MLPs are the wrong learner.
- Design: validation set, 229,964-molecule pool and mass windows IDENTICAL to all earlier runs. Nested training tiers (rows / molecules): A25 15,423 / 6,068; A50 30,818 / 12,137; A 61,701 / 24,274 (= everything so far); B 139,679 / 54,092 (per-source cap 15k molecules); D 633,198 / 235,854 (EVERY cleaned molecule not in validation, <=3 spectra each). Extra molecules canonicalised (metric recipe) and DROPPED if canonical key or stereo-free Murcko scaffold is in validation or key is already in A (11,698 molecules dropped; asserted zero overlap; A block of the new features is bit-identical to the cached v0.2c features and Ridge tier A reproduces v0.2c predictions exactly, max diff 0.0). Same v0.2c sparse features (32,019-column space; empty columns dropped per tier). Models: Ridge alpha 1 (NormalEqRidge); plain MLP 1024x1024 dropout 0.5 (the P5 official config); deep = Linear(1024) -> 4 pre-norm residual blocks (LayerNorm, dropout 0.3) -> LayerNorm -> 1,024 outputs. BCE, AdamW, inner-holdout (scaffold-grouped 10% of the tier) early stopping, one seed.
- Benchmark MRR@25, enveda-180 val (primary, n=2,818) / mixed val (n=2,000):
  Ridge: A25 0.3513/0.4945 | A50 0.3762/0.5170 | A 0.4084/0.5425 | B 0.4842/0.5780 | D 0.5701/0.5871.
  MLP 1024x1024: A25 0.2523/0.4102 | A50 0.2694/0.4374 | A 0.2832/0.4542 | B 0.3903/0.5443 | D 0.5951/0.6062.

- Paired molecule-clustered bootstrap (95% CI): MLP minus Ridge: A25 -0.099, A50 -0.107, A -0.125, B -0.094, **D +0.0250 [+0.0103, +0.0394]** (enveda-180); mixed D +0.0191 [+0.0064, +0.0317]. The MLP learns far faster from data than Ridge (D vs A: +0.31 vs +0.16 enveda-180) and overtakes it between 140k and 633k spectra. The v0.3 verdict (MLP < Ridge) was an artefact of the small training set.
- CAVEATS (read before trusting the headline): (1) At tier D every enveda-180 val molecule has a training analogue at Tanimoto >= 0.4 (1,616 of 2,812 at >= 0.6; none below 0.4), so that slice now measures near-neighbour recognition in a combinatorial library. (2) For non-enveda val molecules, deep minus Ridge at D is +0.063 [+0.036, +0.093] (0.3-0.4, n=811), +0.052 (0.4-0.5), +0.050 (0.5-0.6), +0.058 (>= 0.6), but +0.015 [-0.056, +0.070] for < 0.3 (n=97): too few truly novel molecules to confirm a gain for novel chemistry. (3) The benchmark pool's 200k decoys come from the unused molecules, so at tier D most decoys are also TRAINING molecules; this does not leak the validation truth but it does differ from the test situation. (4) One seed per run: the bootstrap covers validation-query sampling, not training randomness. (5) MLP hyper-parameters were chosen at tier A (inner-holdout BCE) and not re-tuned at D; early stopping on BCE, not on ranking. (6) Tier D uses <=3 spectra per molecule (633k of 1.73M cleaned spectra).
- Cost: Ridge D 124 min (30,769 columns; 7.6 GB Gram matrix; the machine swapped when the MLP queue ran at the same time, so fits were serialised), plain MLP D 54 min, deep D 50 min on MPS (~145 s/epoch); features/data build ~15 min. Artifacts: artifacts/scale (1.6 GB: X_train.npz, rows_train.parquet with tier flags, mol_fp.npy, pred_*.npy, scale_*.csv).

The comparison to v0.2c is NOT a one-change version (data size + learner), but both effects are attributed separately above. ### Pre-P6 check — pool a molecule's spectra into ONE query (2026-10-20; no training)
- Why: the submission needs one ranking per molecule_id (test: 1-9 spectra, mixed adducts/CEs) but every benchmark so far scored single spectra.
- Method: query = mean of the predicted fingerprints of the molecule's spectra (unit length); mass window = intersection of the spectra's P3 windows (union if disjoint). Compared with the mean per-spectrum reciprocal rank, on the 1,034 enveda-180 validation molecules (2,818 spectra, 336 molecules with >1 adduct).
- Result (pooled vs per-spectrum MRR@25): Ridge tier A 0.4274 vs 0.4018 (+0.0255); Ridge tier D 0.5955 vs 0.5624 (+0.0331); deep MLP tier D **0.7314 vs 0.6695 (+0.0619)**. Zero empty window intersections; window misses 0.97%. Pooling gains grow with model quality.
- Decision: pool by molecule in the submission pipeline (kaggle/casmi_submission.ipynb). Benchmarks from P6 on should also report molecule-level MRR.

### P7d — leaderboard result of the PubChem channel (2026-10-21)
- Run: casmi_infer, USE_TIER=True, POP_WEIGHT=0.3, TIER_LIB_BONUS=2.0, frozen deep MLP, library 684,568. 26 min on Kaggle (CPU, 4 cores; 3.9-5.8 s per molecule, 47.1% of guesses from PubChem, min 17 guesses for some molecule).
- Result: **LB 0.142**. Same-weight comparison (the right one): tier off at w=0.30 scored 0.140, so the channel adds **+0.002** (noise). Versus the best run (w=0.15, tier off, 0.146) it is -0.004.
- Reading: the local benchmark predicted +0.03..+0.05 (p=0.4) or +0.01..+0.02 (p=0.2) for these settings; +0.002 means the PubChem channel contributes ~nothing on the hidden test. Probable reasons (not separated): (1) the benchmark's B scenario is optimistic: validation truths are well-documented known compounds whose near neighbours are in train, so the fingerprint cosine separates them better than it will for novel natural products; (2) fewer hidden molecules are in PubChem than assumed (some "never isolated"); (3) with thousands of same-mass isomers the cosine of our MLP cannot rank the answer into the top 25.
- Noise level: with ~400 hidden molecules and MRR ~0.14 the per-molecule reciprocal rank has sd ~0.28, so one score has a standard error of ~0.014 (paired differences between similar rankings are smaller, but not by a lot). The differences among 0.140 / 0.142 / 0.146 are NOT distinguishable. Do not spend submissions on weight tweaks of this size.
- Key diagnosis (from others/ notebook version ladder): public pipelines on essentially the SAME pool (train + COCONUT, 710,701) already reach LB ~0.35 (FPNet engine bank 0.354, + ICEBERG re-scoring 0.358, + ICEBERG/GLACIER lambda 1.0 0.380, then 0.43-0.456 with a PubChem channel, gating and popularity). We reach 0.146 on 684,568. So the gap is the RANKER/ENGINE, not the pool. Our spectrum -> fingerprint MLP was validated on molecules that have close analogues in train; COCONUT-type molecules do not.
- Decision: PubChem channel stays implemented but OFF (USE_TIER=False). Best submission remains w=0.15, tier off, 0.146 (Kaggle submission of casmi_infer). Next: window-calibration probe, then a ranker upgrade (public pretrained models / analog propagation).
